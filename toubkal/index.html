      Sources and notes

      What is measured and what is modelled. Toubkal National Park publishes no visitor counters, no open weather-station feed and no open river gauges, so nothing on this page is a field measurement. Weather comes from the Open-Meteo forecast archive (global and regional models, hourly); river flow from GloFAS, the Copernicus flood model, read through the Open-Meteo Flood API (daily values, joined by a straight line). Both are read live by your browser when the page opens.

      Model points. Each of the 14 points is the nearest model cell, with the temperature corrected to the height of the point. The grid is several kilometres wide, so neighbours such as the Toubkal refuge and the summit share the same rain and wind and differ only in temperature. Wind is the 10 m wind in the valleys, the 700 hPa wind (about 3,100 m) at Tizi n'Tacheddirt and the 600 hPa wind (about 4,300 m) on Toubkal, Ouanoukrim and Tizi n'Ouanoums. Rain turns to snow in the scene when the corrected temperature is 1 °C or less.

      Walking conditions, 0 to 100. A screening index worked out every hour from the model: 100 × wind × cold × rain × cloud. Wind counts in full up to 5 m/s and falls to nothing at 20 m/s; felt temperature (wind chill) counts in full from −5 to 28 °C and falls to nothing at −25 °C and at 40 °C; precipitation falls to nothing at 4 mm/h; full cloud takes off a quarter. An hour is “good” when it is daylight and the index is 70 or more, “fair” from 40. Beam height: the mean of the day's daylight hours; colour: good, fair or poor now. The index knows nothing of snow and ice underfoot, avalanche, rockfall, river crossings or your own fitness. It is not a mountain forecast and not safety advice.

      Illustrations. Hikers, cars and sparks are drawn, not counted: hikers follow daylight and the walking index of the route, cars follow daylight. Nobody publishes how many people walk these paths or when the Oukaïmeden lifts run. Snow on the ground is the model's snow depth spread by elevation, not a satellite image.

      Rivers. GloFAS works on cells of about 5 km. On catchments this small it follows the rise and fall of a river but its absolute discharge is rough. The level on each staff is the flow against the median of the last 90 days, which sits at mid-height.

      Map. Copernicus GLO-30 elevation, drawn every 100 m, heights stretched 1.5×. Park boundary, roads, paths, rivers, lakes, lifts and peaks from Overture Maps (OpenStreetMap data), release . Walking routes are the shortest way along mapped paths between the named points. Times are Morocco standard time (UTC+1; clocks go back one hour during Ramadan). The sun and its shadows are computed for the centre of the map.

      Ni compteurs de visiteurs, ni stations météo ou hydrométriques ouvertes au parc national de Toubkal : rien ici n'est une mesure de terrain. La météo vient de l'archive de prévisions Open-Meteo (horaire), les débits du modèle GloFAS de Copernicus (journalier). L'indice de marche, de 0 à 100, combine chaque heure le vent, le froid ressenti, les précipitations et la nébulosité ; une heure est « favorable » de jour à partir de 70. Il ignore la neige et la glace au sol, les avalanches et les chutes de pierres : ce n'est ni un bulletin de montagne ni un conseil de sécurité. Randonneurs, voitures et étincelles sont des illustrations. Heure du Maroc (UTC+1).
