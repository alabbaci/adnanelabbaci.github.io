Dakhla Watch — how it works

A live dashboard for Dakhla, its peninsula and the lagoon behind it: a low, narrow strip of desert between the open Atlantic and a sheltered bay, where the hazards are wind, swell, Saharan heat and dust, and the rare downpour.

What is measured, what is modelled

  • Peninsula, lagoon, sectors, shoreline — cut from the Overture Maps coastline along the axis of the peninsula. The peninsula covers km², the lagoon km².
  • Elevation, spine profile — Copernicus GLO-30 elevation model.
  • Wind, temperature, rain — Open-Meteo forecast, one point per sector. Model output, not station readings.
  • Waves, swell, sea temperature, tide — Open-Meteo marine models at one open-Atlantic point west of the city. The lagoon is sheltered and is not modelled.
  • Dust, PM10, aerosol — CAMS global atmospheric composition forecast via Open-Meteo.
  • Earthquakes — USGS catalogue, live.
  • Satellite — NASA GIBS daily sea-surface temperature (MUR) and MODIS true colour.
  • Localities, roads, buildings — Overture Maps. “Near shore” buildings lie within m of a shoreline.

Watch levels

Each sector is screened on the forecast for the next 48 hours. The level is the highest that any rule reaches; the wave rule applies to the sectors facing the open Atlantic.

These are screening thresholds chosen for an exposed, arid coast. They are not calibrated against stations and are not an official warning. For decisions, follow the vigilance bulletins of the Direction Générale de la Météorologie and the port authority’s notices.
